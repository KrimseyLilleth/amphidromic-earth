Honest notes

A 0.5° grid (about 55 km) misses points in narrow seas and every "degenerate" point on land (S Norway, E Ireland, New Zealand), because the grid has no water there. Points under 100 km from land are the least reliable.

Two points of opposite rotation very close together are often noise from the grid, not real. They are hidden unless you tick the box.

The animation starts at an arbitrary moment in the cycle; it is not today's real tide. Phases are correct relative to each other.

Currents are calculated, not measured: the slope of the tide surface pushes the water, and Earth's rotation turns it (linear, frictionless). That is good in the deep ocean; in shallow seas friction slows the real flow, so treat those numbers as upper limits. Near the "critical latitude" (where the tide's period matches the rotation's turning period: 74.5° for M2, about 30° for K1/O1) the estimate breaks down and is not shown.

Depth: NOAA ETOPO1, averaged to 0.5°.

Data: R. Ray, GOT4.10c, NASA Goddard. Points found by find_amphidromes.py.
